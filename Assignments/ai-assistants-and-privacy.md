# Using the Course AI Assistants

STAT UN1001 provides two assistants for mini-project work:

- [Data Search and Retrieval Assistant](https://pingpong.hks.harvard.edu/group/735?assistant=3600): helps you locate and retrieve weather variables, environmental sensor data, disaster-response data, and 311 complaint data.
- [Project Feedback Assistant](https://pingpong.hks.harvard.edu/group/735?assistant=3591): gives formative feedback on statistical questions, data descriptions, analyses, graphs, interpretations, and limitations.

The assistants use research tools developed through [Learning the Earth with Artificial Intelligence and Physics (LEAP)](https://leap.columbia.edu/), an NSF Science and Technology Center. LEAP works to improve near-term climate projections by combining data, machine learning, physical modeling, and climate-science expertise. Professor Tian Zheng is LEAP's Deputy Director, Chief Convergence Officer, and Education Director.

## Privacy and Evaluation

Your use of these assistants is anonymous.

- The course and instructional team will not collect or retain your assistant conversations.
- Assistant interaction histories will not be used for grades, assessment, participation tracking, research, or any other evaluation.
- Do not submit prompts, screenshots, conversation transcripts, or records of how often you used an assistant.
- Do not enter your name, UNI, email address, contact information, private information, or confidential data.
- Your grade is based only on the statistical work you intentionally submit.

You do not need to acknowledge or document use of these two course-provided assistants. Meaningful use of other AI tools must follow the course AI policy.

## What You Must Check

The Data Search and Retrieval Assistant helps you find data; it does not guarantee that the data are correct or appropriate for your question. Before using retrieved data, check:

1. Source and provenance: Who produced the data, and how were they collected?
2. Cases: What does each row or observation represent?
3. Variables and units: What is measured, constructed, counted, or classified?
4. Coverage: Which places, dates, time intervals, sensors, events, or complaint types are included?
5. Missingness: What is absent, unavailable, filtered, or unknown?
6. Representation: What population or process can the data reasonably represent?
7. Plausibility: Do ranges, counts, dates, units, and patterns make substantive sense?
8. Claim limits: What conclusions can these data support, and what would overreach?

Treat feedback from the Project Feedback Assistant as advice to evaluate—not as a grade or an authoritative answer. You decide what to accept, revise, or reject.

## Improving the Assistants

At selected in-class checkpoints, the class will contribute anonymous, ungraded feedback about:

- Retrieval failures or irrelevant results.
- Missing datasets, variables, locations, or time periods.
- Incorrect or unclear definitions and units.
- Confusing, overly confident, or statistically weak feedback.
- Capabilities or explanations that would make the assistants more useful.

This improvement feedback is separate from your assignment submission, is not linked to your identity, and is not used for course evaluation.
