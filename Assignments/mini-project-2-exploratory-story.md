# Mini-project 2: Exploratory Data Story

**Due:** Thursday, October 29, 11:59 p.m.

**Value:** 10 points

## Purpose

Use appropriate visual and numerical summaries to describe a pattern in retrieved data without overclaiming what the pattern means.

## In-Class Work

1. Begin with data that passed the Mini-project 1 retrieval audit.
2. Identify the variable or relationship you want to describe.
3. Choose a graph appropriate for the variable types and question.
4. Calculate at least one appropriate numerical summary.
5. Describe the pattern using context, center or proportion, variability, shape, comparisons, and unusual observations as relevant.
6. Write a careful claim and a limitation. Do not use causal language unless the study design supports it.
7. During the Week 07-08 workshops, use Gemini Notebook with your approved source notes and project materials to organize definitions, compare possible displays, and identify claims that need verification. Treat its suggestions as drafts, not as authoritative analysis.
8. Ask the [Project Feedback Assistant](https://pingpong.hks.harvard.edu/group/735?assistant=3591) for formative feedback if useful. Evaluate the suggestions and revise using your own judgment.

## Submit

Submit:

- Statistical question.
- One clearly labeled graph.
- At least one numerical summary.
- A short interpretation in context.
- One limitation or alternative explanation.
- Data-source citation.

Submit the revised statistical work—not prompts, screenshots, feedback transcripts, or a record of whether you used the assistant.

For the in-class Gemini Notebook workshop, do not enter unnecessary identifying information, private information, or confidential data. Check every suggested definition, calculation, graph, and claim against the data and its documentation. Gemini Notebook use and assistant use are not graded; the submitted statistical artifact is what matters.

## Grading rubric (10 points)

This project is graded generously for thoughtful, course-appropriate work, but it is not graded for submission alone. A student can earn full credit with a simple analysis when the choices are appropriate, the reasoning is visible, and the interpretation is accurate.

| Criterion | Points | Full-credit description |
|---|---:|---|
| Question, data, and method align | 2.0 | Clearly identifies the statistical question, what one row/case represents, the relevant variable(s), and why the chosen graph and summary address the question. |
| Visualization choice and design | 2.5 | Uses an appropriate display for the variable type and question; labels axes/units and categories; makes the main pattern readable; avoids misleading scales or decoration. |
| Numerical summary | 2.0 | Includes at least one appropriate numerical summary, computed or transcribed accurately, with enough information for a reader to understand what it summarizes. |
| Interpretation in context | 2.5 | Correctly describes the pattern, center/proportion, spread, comparison, or unusual values as relevant; separates description from causation and does not overclaim. |
| Clarity, limitation, and source verification | 1.0 | Is organized and readable, cites the data source, names a meaningful limitation or alternative explanation, and shows that definitions and AI-supplied raw ingredients were checked against the source/data. |

## How Gemini Notebook fits the rubric

Gemini Notebook may help organize source notes, identify variable definitions, and suggest candidate graphs or summaries. It supplies raw ingredients, not a finished analysis. You are responsible for choosing the display and summary, checking the data, doing or verifying the calculation, and writing the interpretation. Gemini Notebook use and assistant-use histories are not graded.
