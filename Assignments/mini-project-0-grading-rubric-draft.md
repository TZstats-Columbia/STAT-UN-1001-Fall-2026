# Mini-project 0 grading rubric draft

Suggested total: 10 points. Half-points are appropriate.

This rubric is meant to be generous, but not completion-only. Award strong credit when the student gives a clear, usable description of the retrieved data and shows that they understand what the data can and cannot answer. Do not penalize small formatting differences or minor wording issues if the statistical meaning is clear.

## 1. Statistical question and revision: 2 points

- 2.0: Gives both an original and revised question. The revised question is specific enough to investigate with the retrieved data and appropriately narrows the original question when needed.
- 1.5: Gives both questions, and the revised question is mostly data-investigable, but one element such as time, place, population, or variable is still somewhat vague.
- 1.0: Gives a reasonable original question, but the revision is weak, missing, or only slightly connected to the available data.
- 0.5: Question is present but very broad, not clearly statistical, or poorly matched to the data.
- 0: No meaningful statistical question.

## 2. Data source and relevance: 1.5 points

- 1.5: Names a plausible dataset or data source and the data are relevant to the revised question.
- 1.0: Data source is identified but not fully specific, or the connection to the revised question is only partly clear.
- 0.5: Mentions a general source or assistant output, but the actual dataset/subset is hard to identify.
- 0: No dataset or clearly irrelevant data source.

## 3. Cases, variables, types, and units: 3 points

- 3.0: Clearly identifies the cases/rows and the main variables. Variable types are mostly correct, and units or values are included when relevant.
- 2.5: Cases and main variables are clear, with only minor errors or omissions in type labels, units, or values.
- 2.0: Cases and variables are understandable, but there is some confusion about the unit of observation, aggregation level, variable type, or units.
- 1.0: Some variables are listed, but cases are missing or substantially confused, or key variables needed for the question are omitted.
- 0.5: Minimal mention of data elements, but not enough to understand the dataset.
- 0: No meaningful description of cases or variables.

## 4. Population, place, and time period: 1.5 points

- 1.5: Clearly states the relevant population or process, geographic coverage, and time period, and these match the retrieved data.
- 1.0: Includes all three elements, but one is too broad or somewhat mismatched to the data.
- 0.5: Includes only one or two of the three elements, or gives them in a very vague way.
- 0: Population/place/time are missing or not interpretable.

## 5. Important limitation and correct interpretation: 1.5 points

- 1.5: Gives a meaningful limitation that shows why the retrieved data may not fully answer the original question. The limitation is specific to the dataset, such as reporting bias, proxy measurement, missing denominator, one-station coverage, short time period, aggregation, missingness, or lack of causal comparison.
- 1.0: Gives a real limitation, but it is somewhat generic or not fully connected to the dataset.
- 0.5: Mentions a limitation, but it is vague, minor, or not clearly relevant.
- 0: No limitation, or the interpretation seriously overclaims what the data can show.

## 6. Clarity and organization: 0.5 points

- 0.5: Submission is easy to follow and the requested pieces can be located without difficulty.
- 0.25: Mostly understandable, but disorganized or repetitive.
- 0: Too unclear to grade reliably.

## Suggested grade bands

- 9-10: Strong answer. Complete, clear, and mostly correct; minor imprecision is fine.
- 8-8.5: Solid answer. Most requested pieces are present and understandable; a few concepts may be imprecise.
- 6.5-7.5: Adequate answer. Good-faith work with recognizable data description, but several pieces are vague, incomplete, or somewhat mismatched.
- 5-6: Partial answer. Some important pieces are present, but the dataset description is hard to understand or contains major confusion.
- Below 5: Minimal, off-target, or missing major required components.

## Notes for generous but substantive grading

- Do not require a perfect dataset. Reward students who recognize that the retrieved data only supports a narrower question.
- Do not require expert-level metadata. For Mini-project 0, students should identify the main cases, variables, units, coverage, and limitation well enough that another reader can understand the dataset.
- Small mistakes in variable type labels should not dominate the grade if the data description is otherwise clear.
- The most common issue to watch for is overclaiming the population. For example, 311 complaints are records of reported complaints, not necessarily all real-world incidents; one weather station is not all of New York City; a short time window does not support long-term trend claims.
- A submission should not receive full credit for mere completion if cases/variables are unclear, if the data source is not identifiable, or if the revised question is not connected to the retrieved data.

## Quick comment templates

- Strong answer: "Clear and thoughtful data description. Your revised question is well matched to the available data, and your limitation shows good awareness of what the dataset can and cannot support."
- Mostly strong with imprecision: "Good work overall. The main pieces are here; the main area to tighten is the distinction between the real-world population/process and what the dataset actually records."
- Cases/variables issue: "The dataset source is relevant, but the unit of observation needs to be clearer. Please specify what one row/case represents and then list the variables measured for that row."
- Limitation issue: "The limitation should be more specific to this dataset. A strong limitation would explain whether the data are a proxy, have incomplete coverage, miss a denominator, or cannot support a causal claim."
- Question-data mismatch: "The original question is interesting, but the retrieved data support a narrower revised question. The revised question should match the actual variables, place, and time period in the dataset."
