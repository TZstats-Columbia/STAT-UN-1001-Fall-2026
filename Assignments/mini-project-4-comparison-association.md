# Mini-project 4: Comparison or Association

## Purpose

Use retrieved data to make and critique a comparison or association claim while distinguishing statistical evidence from causal explanation.

## In-Class Work

1. Formulate a question comparing groups, places, complaint types, events, or time periods, or examining an association between two variables.
2. Use the [Data Search and Retrieval Assistant](https://pingpong.hks.harvard.edu/group/735?assistant=3600) to retrieve or refine the required data.
3. Recheck provenance, definitions, units, coverage, missingness, aggregation, and whether groups are comparable.
4. Choose an appropriate graph, numerical summary, interval, test, or regression-based method.
5. State the statistical result in context and distinguish statistical significance from practical importance when relevant.
6. Identify confounding, selection, measurement, temporal, geographic, or aggregation limitations.
7. Ask the [Project Feedback Assistant](https://pingpong.hks.harvard.edu/group/735?assistant=3591) to critique the claim if useful. Decide which suggestions are statistically justified and revise the artifact yourself.

## Submit

Submit:

- Statistical question.
- Data source and comparison or association design.
- One appropriate graph or table.
- Relevant numerical or inferential result.
- A careful conclusion in context.
- At least two limitations, including why the result does or does not support a causal conclusion.

Submit the revised statistical artifact—not prompts, screenshots, conversation histories, or a record of assistant use.

## Completion Criteria

Full completion credit requires an appropriate comparison or association, a defensible conclusion, and limitations that address design and interpretation. Assistant use itself is not graded.
