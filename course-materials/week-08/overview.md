# Week 08 Overview: Confidence intervals and decision errors

Dates: Tuesday, Oct 27 and Thursday, Oct 29

## Focus

This week develops the logic of inference. We begin with a sample proportion's sampling distribution and use it to distinguish a fixed population parameter from a statistic that changes across random samples. We then use confidence intervals to communicate what a sample says, with what precision, and under what modeling assumptions.

Thursday emphasizes guided computation and interpretation. We will practice the confidence-interval setup in Example 5.8 and Guided Practices 5.9 and 5.14, then introduce decision errors through Guided Practice 5.17. Quiz 3 takes place during the final 15 minutes of class. The Week 08 confidence-interval and decision-error material prepares students for later work; it is not part of Quiz 3.

## Learning goals

By the end of the week, students should be able to:

- Identify the chance process, possible values, center, and standard error for a sample proportion.
- Distinguish a fixed population parameter from a sample statistic that changes across repeated samples.
- Check when the approximate normal model for a sample proportion is appropriate.
- Construct and interpret a confidence interval using a point estimate, a critical value, and a standard error.
- Explain the repeated-sampling meaning of a 95% confidence interval without treating the fixed parameter as random.
- Explain how confidence level, standard error, and margin of error affect an interval's width.
- State a null and alternative hypothesis in a simple context and distinguish Type I from Type II decision errors.
- Separate precision from bias and other threats to validity.
