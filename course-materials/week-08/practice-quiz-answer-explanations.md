# Week 08 Practice Quiz: Answer Explanations

These explanations are provided for review after an attempt.

1. **Model.** Under the conditions, the sampling distribution of `p-hat` is approximately normal, centered at the population proportion `p`. The statistic changes from sample to sample; the parameter is fixed.
2. **Conditions.** `n p-hat = 1000(0.887)=887` and `n(1-p-hat)=1000(0.113)=113`. Both are at least 10, so the success-failure condition supports the normal approximation. The randomization/independence condition is also needed.
3. **Figure 5.6 count.** 24 of the 25 intervals capture `p = 0.88`; one misses.
4. **Figure 5.6 lesson.** A 95% method captures the fixed parameter in about 95% of repeated samples, so an occasional miss is expected and does not disprove the method.
5. **Confidence level.** A 99% method must capture the parameter more often in repeated sampling, so it uses a larger critical value. With the same standard error, `ME=z*×SE` is larger and the interval is wider.
6. **Margin of error.** `ME=1.96(0.010)=0.0196`, or about 1.96 percentage points.
7. **Endpoints.** Lower: `0.887-0.0196=0.8674`. Upper: `0.887+0.0196=0.9066`. The interval is `(0.8674, 0.9066)`, or about 86.7% to 90.7%.
8. **Interpretation.** We are 95% confident that the true proportion of U.S. adults who support the policy is between about 86.7% and 90.7%. The confidence level describes the repeated method, not a probability attached to this one fixed interval.
9. **Guided Practice 5.9.** A normal model has about 99% of its area within ±2.58 standard deviations. The larger multiplier makes the 99% margin of error and interval width larger than the corresponding 95% interval.
10. **Guided Practice 5.14.** In repeated sampling, about 95% of intervals made by this method would capture the population parameter. An older poll may not describe current opinions because the target population or opinions may have changed; a larger old sample does not solve that validity problem.
11. **Guided Practice 5.17.** `H0` is “innocent,” `HA` is “guilty.” Failing to reject `H0` means the evidence is not strong enough to convict; it does not prove innocence.
12. **Errors.** Type I: reject a true null (convict an innocent person). Type II: fail to reject a false null (fail to convict a guilty person).
13. **Bias versus variability.** A larger sample usually reduces random sampling variability, but it does not repair a sampling process that systematically misses part of the population.
