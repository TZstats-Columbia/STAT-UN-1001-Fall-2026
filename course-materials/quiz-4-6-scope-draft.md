# Draft Quiz Scope Note: Quizzes 4, 5, and 6

These scopes are based on the current projected lecture plan. The emphasis will be on statistical reasoning, model setup, interpretation, and course-scope calculations—not memorizing practice-quiz numbers or carrying out long arithmetic.

All quizzes are 15 minutes. You may bring one single-sided page of notes.

## Quiz 4 — Lectures 14–17

Quiz 4 will focus on the foundations of confidence intervals and hypothesis testing:

- sampling distributions for sample proportions, model conditions, and standard error;
- the sample mean as a random variable and the difference between the standard deviation of individual values and the standard error of a statistic;
- population parameters versus sample statistics and point estimates;
- confidence intervals, confidence level, critical value, margin of error, and interval width;
- interpreting Figure 5.6 and Example 5.8 in context;
- the repeated-sampling meaning of confidence;
- two-sided null and alternative hypotheses for Quiz 4; evidence against a chance model in either direction; test statistics; and p-value meaning;
- Type I and Type II decision errors; and
- statistical significance versus practical importance.

You should be able to identify an incorrect interpretation, explain what a confidence interval or hypothesis test does and does not establish, and distinguish precision from bias or other threats to validity.

## Quiz 5 — Lectures 17–20

Quiz 5 will focus on one-sample inference for a population mean, together with method choice in the project workshop:

- distinguishing a numerical outcome and population mean `mu` from a categorical outcome and population proportion `p`;
- the sampling distribution of a sample mean, estimated standard error, and the role of the `t` distribution when the population standard deviation is unknown;
- constructing and interpreting a confidence interval for one population mean;
- setting up and interpreting a one-sample hypothesis test for one population mean, including a p-value and a careful conclusion;
- checking conditions, recognizing the role of outliers or a strongly skewed sample, and distinguishing precision from bias or other design limitations;
- distinguishing statistical evidence from causal explanation;
- identifying the population, sample, parameter, statistic, and estimand in a project-style question;
- selecting whether a one-proportion or one-mean procedure fits a project-style question; and
- communicating uncertainty, practical importance, data-quality concerns, and limitations.

The project workshop itself is not tested as Gemini Notebook knowledge. You are responsible for the statistical reasoning and calculations, not for reproducing an assistant's output. Two-proportion, two-mean, paired-mean, and chi-square procedures are outside the projected Quiz 5 scope.

## Quiz 6 — Lectures 21–23

Quiz 6 will focus on correlation, regression, and responsible statistical communication:

- interpreting the direction and strength of correlation;
- interpreting slope, fitted values, and residuals in context;
- recognizing outliers, leverage, and extrapolation risks;
- distinguishing association, prediction, and causation;
- identifying when a regression or prediction claim overreaches the data;
- communicating uncertainty, limitations, and practical meaning; and
- integrating design, description, inference, and regression ideas in a concise conclusion.

You should be able to explain what a regression result supports, what it does not support, and how the study design affects the claim.
