# Week 10 Practice Quiz: Comparing Proportions and Chi-Square Reasoning

This is a 0-point practice quiz with unlimited attempts and immediate feedback. Try each question with your own reasoning before selecting **Just show me the answer.** The anonymous preparation check-in, not this quiz, earns the 10 completion points.

## Required reading

- OpenIntro 6.2.1–6.2.3; use 6.2.4 for interpretation. Section 6.2.5 is not required.
- OpenIntro 6.3.1, 6.3.2, and 6.3.5. Sections 6.3.3 and 6.3.4 are not required.
- OpenIntro 6.4.1–6.4.2.

## Quiz emphasis

### Comparing two proportions

1. Define the order and interpret a difference of sample proportions.
2. Interpret a confidence interval for `p1 - p2`, including the role of zero.
3. Identify the design and success-failure conditions.
4. Explain what contributes to the standard error without computing it.
5. State hypotheses and interpret a supplied p-value.
6. Explain why the null-model calculation uses a common pooled proportion.

### Goodness of fit

7. Recognize a goodness-of-fit question.
8. Calculate expected counts from null proportions.
9. Compare observed and expected counts.
10. Explain the meaning of a chi-square contribution and the overall statistic.
11. Interpret a supplied chi-square p-value and conclusion.

### Independence in a two-way table

12. Recognize an independence question involving two categorical variables.
13. Calculate selected expected counts from row, column, and grand totals.
14. State independence hypotheses.
15. Use observed-versus-expected patterns and a supplied p-value to reason about association.
16. Separate evidence of association from a causal conclusion.
17. Read an observed-versus-expected pattern in selected cells of a two-way table.

### News-story concept checks

18. Use a Florida State University story about revised hurricane maps to identify a two-proportion comparison and separate comprehension from intended action and actual behavior.
19. Use a Pew Research Center story about age and trust in information to recognize a chi-square independence question involving age group and a four-category trust response.

### Method choice and synthesis

20. Distinguish a two-proportion comparison, goodness-of-fit test, and independence test.

No question requires a full hand calculation of a chi-square statistic or a chi-square table lookup.
