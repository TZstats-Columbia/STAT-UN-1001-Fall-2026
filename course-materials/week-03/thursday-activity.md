# Week 03 Thursday Plan: Project Discussion and Work Session

Date: September 24, 2026  
Class time: 10:10–11:25 a.m.  
Goal: Use the full meeting for Mini-project 0 discussion and Mini-project 1 development. Do not introduce new Chapter 2 concepts.

## 10:10–10:18 | Whole-class project orientation

- Reconnect the project to the statistical workflow: question → data → cases and variables → provenance → limitations → defensible claim.
- Clarify the difference between a topic and a statistical question.
- Remind students that assistant conversations are not submitted or evaluated.

## 10:18–10:30 | Project discussion

Invite several students to share a question or retrieval challenge without sharing private assistant conversations.

Discuss:

- What is the observational unit?
- What population or process does the question concern?
- Which variables are needed?
- Are they numerical, categorical, time-indexed, or geographic?
- What does the retrieved source actually measure?
- What important thing might the data omit or misrepresent?

Record recurring issues for the class: unavailable variables, unclear units, geographic mismatch, temporal mismatch, proxy measurements, missingness, and questions too broad for the available data.

## 10:30–11:08 | Structured project work

Students work individually or in pairs on one of two paths.

### Path A: Complete or revise Mini-project 0

- State one statistical question.
- Identify cases, variables, target population, and time/geographic scope.
- Retrieve or locate a plausible dataset.
- State one limitation that follows from how the data are measured or selected.

### Path B: Begin Mini-project 1 data biography

- Identify the original producer and source URL.
- Record variable definitions and units.
- Record geographic and temporal coverage.
- Examine missingness and reporting/measurement processes.
- Write what the data can and cannot represent.

Instructor and TAs hold short consultations, prioritizing students whose retrieval failed or whose question does not match the available variables.

## 11:08–11:20 | Small-group critique and revision

Each student explains:

1. the question;
2. the cases and variables;
3. the strongest limitation.

Partners check whether the proposed claim matches the study design and data source. Students revise one sentence before leaving the group.

## 11:20–11:25 | Share-out and next step

- Collect two examples of a question that became more precise.
- Name one common data limitation the class should continue watching.
- Each student records the next concrete project action and where they will seek help if blocked.
