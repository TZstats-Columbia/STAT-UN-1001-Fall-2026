## Question 1. Figure 2.2 – What makes the relationship nonlinear?

The rate of decrease changes: the pattern is steep at first and then flattens, so one straight line would not follow it well.

## Question 2. Exercise 2.2 practice – Types of association

Correct answer:
A: positive linear; B: negative linear; C: nonlinear; D: no clear association

Why:
Direction describes whether y tends to rise or fall as x rises. Linearity asks whether a straight line describes the pattern. Plot C has a strong association even though its overall direction is neither simply positive nor negative.

## Question 3. Mean notation – What does n mean?

In x̄ = (x1 + x2 + ... + xn) / n, what does n represent?

Correct answer:
The number of observations in the sample

Why:
The subscripts label observations, from the first value x1 through the nth value xn. The denominator n is the number of observed values being averaged. See Guided Practices 2.4 and 2.5.

## Question 4. Five-number mean practice

Compute the mean of the five values 1, 2, 1, 2, 4.

Correct answer:
2

Why:
Add the values: 1 + 2 + 1 + 2 + 4 = 10. There are n = 5 values, so the mean is 10/5 = 2.

## Question 5. Binary variables – The sum is a count

Eight service requests are coded 1 if resolved within one day and 0 otherwise: 1, 0, 1, 1, 0, 0, 1, 1. What does the sum of these values represent?

Correct answer:
The number of requests resolved within one day: 5

Why:
Each case with the characteristic contributes 1 and every other case contributes 0. Adding the indicators therefore counts the cases coded 1.

## Question 6. Binary variables – The mean is a proportion

For the same 0/1 values—1, 0, 1, 1, 0, 0, 1, 1—what is the mean, and what does it represent?

Correct answer:
5/8 = 0.625; the proportion of requests resolved within one day

Why:
The sum is the count of 1s (5). Dividing by all cases (8) gives 5/8 = 0.625. For a 0/1 variable, the mean equals the fraction coded 1.

## Question 7. Figure 2.5 practice – Create binned counts

Values are 5.2, 7.4, 7.5, 8.1, 9.9, 10.0, 10.8, and 12.5. Use bins 5.0–7.5, 7.5–10.0, and 10.0–12.5, placing a boundary value in the lower bin as in Figure 2.5. What are the three counts?

Correct answer:
3, 3, 2

Why:
The first bin contains 5.2, 7.4, and boundary value 7.5; the second contains 8.1, 9.9, and boundary value 10.0; the third contains 10.8 and 12.5. This is the count-table step used to build a histogram.

## Question 11. Exercise 2.12 practice – Read a skewed histogram

## Question 12. Mode practice

Which statement best describes a mode when reading a histogram?

Correct answer:
A prominent peak or concentration in the distribution

Why:
OpenIntro emphasizes prominent peaks rather than tiny bin-to-bin fluctuations. A distribution may be unimodal, bimodal, or multimodal depending on the number of prominent peaks.

## Question 13. Median and quartiles practice

For the ordered values 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, exclude the median when finding the lower and upper quartiles. What are the median, Q1, and Q3?

Correct answer:
Median = 5, Q1 = 2.5, Q3 = 7.5

Why:
The middle value is 5. The lower half is 1, 2, 3, 4, whose median is 2.5; the upper half is 6, 7, 8, 9, whose median is 7.5.

## Question 14. Exercise 2.20 practice – Why use a log scale?

This is not easy; make a good attempt. The panels show the same hypothetical county populations before and after a log transformation. What is the main benefit of panel B?

Correct answer:
It compresses very large values and separates smaller values, making the distribution's structure easier to see.

Why:
A log transformation changes the scale, not which counties are present. It compresses orders of magnitude so that observations hidden in the crowded left side of the raw histogram become visible. This prepares you for Exercise 2.20, which we will discuss in class.

## Question 15. Create a contingency table

Eight students used either a video or text explanation and then rated it helpful (Yes/No): Video–Yes, Video–Yes, Video–No, Video–Yes, Text–Yes, Text–No, Text–No, Text–No. Which 2×2 table is correct? Entries are ordered as Video-Yes, Video-No; Text-Yes, Text-No.

Correct answer:
3, 1; 1, 3

Why:
Count each combination: Video has 3 Yes and 1 No; Text has 1 Yes and 3 No. A contingency table organizes counts for every combination of two categorical variables.

## Question 16. Guided Practice 2.23 – Row proportion

The plots below show 100 individual applications and 50 joint applications. What does 45/100 = 0.45 represent?

Correct answer:
Among individual applications, the proportion from applicants with a mortgage

Why:

## Question 17. Guided Practice 2.23 – Column proportion

Using the same data, what does 45/(45 + 20) represent?

Correct answer:
Among applications from people with mortgages, the proportion that were individual applications

Why:
The denominator 45 + 20 is the mortgage column total. Therefore the fraction conditions on mortgage status and asks what share of that column is individual. This mirrors part (b) of Guided Practice 2.23.

## Question 18. Exercise 2.22-style practice – Conditional proportions

From the explanation table, 3 of 4 video users and 1 of 4 text users rated the explanation helpful. Which conclusion is supported descriptively?

Correct answer:
Helpfulness was associated with explanation format in this small group: 75% for video versus 25% for text.

Why:
Compare conditional proportions within each format: 3/4 versus 1/4. They differ, so the variables are associated in this sample. Counts alone do not establish causation or broad generalization.

## Question 19. Exercise 2.26 practice – Compare transplant outcomes

A hypothetical randomized study recorded 24 survivors and 6 deaths in treatment, and 15 survivors and 15 deaths in control. Which statement is correct?

Correct answer:
Survival was 80% in treatment and 50% in control, a 30-percentage-point difference; random assignment supports a causal treatment comparison.

Why:
Treatment survival is 24/30 = 0.80 and control survival is 15/30 = 0.50. Because the scenario specifies random assignment, the outcome difference can be interpreted as evidence about the treatment effect, subject to ordinary study limitations.

## Question 20. Exercise 2.31-style practice – Nongendered group comparison

The plots compare commute times for two transit routes. Which comparison is best supported?

Correct answer:
Route A has a lower typical commute time and less variability than Route B.

Why:
The dashed median for Route A lies left of Route B's, and Route A's observations occupy a narrower range. A good comparison describes center, spread, shape, and any unusual features—not just one extreme value.
