# Week 11 Overview: Inference for a population mean and project workshop

Dates: Tuesday, November 17 and Thursday, November 19

This week introduces inference for a **population mean**: confidence intervals and hypothesis tests when the outcome is numerical. We will then use the second class meeting to practice choosing between a mean and a proportion method and to apply course-scope reasoning to the merged Mini-project 3.

## Prepare

- Read the assigned portion of OpenIntro Chapter 7 on one-sample inference for a numerical outcome, with special attention to the distinction between a population mean and a sample mean.
- Review the course's confidence-interval and hypothesis-testing workflow from Chapter 5.
- Identify whether the outcome in your project question is numerical or categorical, and name the population, sample, parameter, and statistic.

## In class

**Tuesday, Nov 17: one-sample inference for a population mean**

- Distinguish a numerical outcome and population mean `mu` from a categorical outcome and population proportion `p`.
- Connect the sampling distribution of `x-bar`, its estimated standard error, and the role of the `t` distribution when the population standard deviation is unknown.
- Build and interpret a confidence interval for a population mean; discuss conditions, sample size, unusual observations, and what the interval does and does not address.

**Thursday, Nov 19: practice, method choice, and project workshop**

- Carry out the reasoning steps of a one-sample test for a population mean and interpret a provided test statistic or p-value in context.
- Compare a mean question with a proportion question, choose the matching course method, and state the parameter.
- Bring the Mini-project 2 data/source notes and one possible inferential question. Gemini Notebook may provide raw source documentation, variable definitions, a data extract, and candidate summaries; check those ingredients, then carry out the course-scope calculation yourself.
- Give one peer constructive and curious feedback comment about method fit, uncertainty, or a limitation.

The merged Mini-project 3 is due Tuesday, November 24 at 11:59 p.m.

## Scope boundary

This week covers **one-sample** inference for a population mean. It does not introduce two-sample or paired-mean procedures. Quiz 5 will emphasize method choice, setup, interpretation, and short core calculations rather than long arithmetic.
