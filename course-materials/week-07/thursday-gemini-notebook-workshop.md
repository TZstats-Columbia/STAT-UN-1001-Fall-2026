# Thursday Workshop: Gemini Notebook and Mini-project 2

## Workshop purpose

Use Gemini Notebook as a structured collaborator while building Mini-project 2, an exploratory data story. The goal is not to outsource the analysis. The goal is to make source notes, definitions, questions, and possible explanations easier to inspect and revise.

## Suggested 75-minute plan

1. **5 minutes — Frame the work.** Revisit the Mini-project 2 requirements: statistical question, labeled graph, numerical summary, interpretation in context, limitation, and data-source citation.
2. **10 minutes — Instructor demonstration.** Start with a small set of approved source notes and a simple data question. Demonstrate asking the notebook to summarize the source, identify the unit of observation, propose a graph, and list claims that require verification. Model rejecting an incorrect or overconfident suggestion.
3. **35 minutes — Individual or pair workshop.** Students load their approved project materials and work through the workflow below.
4. **15 minutes — Verification exchange.** A partner asks: “What does one row represent?”, “What is the denominator?”, “Which claim is directly supported?”, and “What could be missing or biased?”
5. **10 minutes — Exit submission.** Students save a draft statistical artifact and a short list of one useful suggestion and one suggestion they rejected or revised.

## Student workflow

### 1. Ground the notebook

Provide the notebook with only the materials appropriate for this course activity: the public data source, data dictionary or documentation, and your own project notes. Do not include names, contact information, private records, or confidential course information.

### 2. Ask for a source-and-data check

Suggested prompt:

> From these sources, summarize who produced the data, what one row/case represents, the variables and units, the geographic and time coverage, missingness, and any warnings about interpretation. Quote or point to the source for each important claim. If the source does not answer something, say that it is unknown.

### 3. Ask for question and display options

Suggested prompt:

> Given my statistical question and these variable definitions, propose two or three exploratory graphs and one numerical summary. For each option, explain what it would show, what it would hide, and what assumptions or data checks I should make. Do not claim causation.

### 4. Verify with the data

Check the notebook's suggestions against the actual file or table:

- Does the proposed graph match the variable types?
- Are the units, dates, categories, and denominators correct?
- Are missing values or filtered cases changing the result?
- Does the numerical summary use the intended cases?
- Does the graph label the population, sample, time period, and units clearly?

### 5. Draft a careful story

Your draft should include:

- the statistical question;
- what the cases and variables are;
- one labeled graph;
- at least one numerical summary;
- an interpretation that describes what the data show;
- a limitation or alternative explanation;
- a source citation.

Use the Project Feedback Assistant only if useful for formative feedback, and evaluate its suggestions. Assistant prompts, screenshots, and conversation histories are not part of the submission.

## Exit check

Before leaving, answer:

1. What did the data directly show?
2. Which statement did you verify against the source or data rather than accept from the notebook?
3. What is one important limitation or missing variable?
4. What is the next revision your Mini-project 2 artifact needs?
