# Week 06 Overview: Bayes, random variables, normal and binomial models, and Quiz 2

Dates: Oct 13 and Oct 15

## Focus

This week starts by finishing important Chapter 3 ideas that we did not fully cover in Week 05: probability trees, Bayes-style reasoning, random variables, expected value, and simple linear combinations of random variables. We then preview only two named distribution models from Chapter 4: the normal distribution and the binomial distribution.

We will not emphasize geometric, negative binomial, or Poisson distributions in this course.

## Learning Goals

By the end of the week, students should be able to:

- Use a probability tree to compute a joint probability, a marginal probability, and a reversed conditional probability.
- Put `P(B | A)` and `P(A | B)` into words and explain why they are different questions.
- Recognize Bayes' rule as a way to reverse conditional probability.
- Identify a random variable and compute the mean or expected value of a simple discrete random variable.
- Use simple linear-combination rules for `aX + b`: shifting changes the mean but not the standard deviation; scaling changes both the mean and the standard deviation.
- Recognize the basic purpose of normal and binomial distributions.
