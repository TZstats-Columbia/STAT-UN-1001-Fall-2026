# Week 06 Overview: Bayes, random variables, normal and binomial distribution models, and Quiz 2

Dates: Oct 13 and Oct 15

## Focus

This week starts by finishing important Chapter 3 ideas that we did not fully cover in Week 05: probability trees, Bayes-style reasoning, random variables, expected value, and simple linear combinations of random variables. We then use Chapter 3.5 and selected Chapter 4 sections to reason with two named distribution models: the normal distribution and the binomial distribution.

We will not emphasize geometric, negative binomial, or Poisson distributions in this course.

## Learning Goals

By the end of the week, students should be able to:

- Use a probability tree to compute a joint probability, a marginal probability, and a reversed conditional probability.
- Put `P(B | A)` and `P(A | B)` into words and explain why they are different questions.
- Recognize Bayes' rule as a way to reverse conditional probability.
- Identify a random variable and compute the mean or expected value of a simple discrete random variable.
- Use simple linear-combination rules for `aX + b`: shifting changes the mean but not the standard deviation; scaling changes both the mean and the standard deviation.
- Use a histogram and fitted normal curve to reason about model fit, area-as-probability, and events such as `P(X > a)`.
- Standardize a value to compare observations from distributions with different means and standard deviations.
- Recognize the basic purpose of the binomial model, compute one exact probability such as `P(X = c)`, and interpret the binomial mean and standard deviation.
