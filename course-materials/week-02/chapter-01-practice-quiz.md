## Student-facing study description: urban rat reports

Researchers investigated whether reported rat activity changed over time in cities and whether those changes were related to warming temperatures. They assembled long-running records from 16 cities. Depending on the city, the records consisted of complaints submitted by residents or official rat-sighting reports. The researchers examined trends in reports over time and compared them with temperature trends and other city characteristics.

The researchers did not randomly assign cities to warmer or cooler conditions, and they did not directly count every rat. Complaint and sighting reports are proxy measurements: they may change when rat activity changes, but they may also change because of reporting apps, public attention, population changes, or differences in city reporting practices.

Source context: [Urban Rat Complaints as a Proxy](../../teaching-stories/urban-rat-complaints-proxy-2026-06-29.md), based on the linked AP News account and underlying study.

## Questions and answer key

### 1. OpenIntro Exercise 1.6: Stealers and study components

In a study, 129 UC Berkeley undergraduates identified themselves as lower or higher social class. Each student could take candies that had been described as intended for children, and each later reported the number taken. Which option correctly identifies the study components?

- **Correct:** The research question concerns an association between perceived social class and candies taken; the subjects are the 129 undergraduates; social class is categorical and number of candies is numerical and discrete.
- The research question concerns whether candy causes social class; the subjects are the children; both variables are continuous.
- The research question concerns the average number of candies in the jar; the subjects are candies; social class is numerical.
- The study has only one variable because every participant saw the same jar.

Feedback: The investigators recorded perceived social class and a count of candies for each of 129 student participants. Social class was not randomly assigned, so the study examines association rather than a causal effect of social class.

### 2. OpenIntro Exercise 1.17: Relaxing after work

A random sample of 1,155 Americans reported how many hours they had to relax after an average workday. The sample mean was 1.65 hours. Which classification is correct?

- **Correct:** A sampled American is an observation; hours of relaxation is a variable; 1.65 is a sample statistic; the mean for all Americans is a population parameter.
- A sampled American is a variable; hours of relaxation is a parameter; 1.65 is an observation; the population mean is a statistic.
- A sampled American is a statistic; hours of relaxation is an observation; 1.65 is a parameter; the population mean is a variable.
- A sampled American is a parameter; hours of relaxation is a statistic; 1.65 is a variable; the population mean is an observation.

Feedback: Observations are the sampled people, the recorded characteristic is the variable, a value calculated from the sample is a statistic, and the corresponding population quantity is a parameter.

### 3. OpenIntro Exercise 1.26: Family size

To estimate household size, a researcher randomly samples elementary-school students and asks each student's family size. What is the main problem with this design?

- **Correct:** It will tend to overestimate average household size because larger households can contribute more students and therefore have more opportunities to enter the sample.
- It will tend to underestimate household size because children always report fewer people than adults.
- It is unbiased because the students were selected randomly from the school.
- It estimates household size exactly because every sampled child represents one household.

Feedback: Random selection from a list of students is not the same as random selection of households. Households with more eligible children are more likely to be represented.

### 4. OpenIntro Exercise 1.30: Vitamin supplements

Researchers assigned 400 university volunteers to placebo or one of three vitamin-C treatments. Tablets looked identical. Participants did not know their treatment, researchers assessing outcomes did not know, but nurses distributing pills did know. Which description is most accurate?

- **Correct:** This is an experiment; treatment group is explanatory; cold duration or severity is the response; participants were blinded, but the study was not fully double-blind because the nurses knew assignments.
- This is observational because participants could choose whether to take their pills; vitamin dose is the response and cold duration is explanatory.
- This is a census because 400 people participated; the study has no explanatory variable.
- This is double-blind because the tablets looked identical, regardless of what the nurses knew.

Feedback: Researchers imposed treatments, making this an experiment. Identical tablets can blind participants, but staff members who know assignments prevent full double-blinding.

### 5. OpenIntro Exercise 1.37: Chia seeds and weight loss

Researchers recruited 38 men and 38 women who volunteered. Within each sex, participants were randomly assigned to receive chia seeds or a placebo. Which conclusion about the design is correct?

- **Correct:** This is a randomized experiment blocked by sex; it can support a causal comparison for participants, but generalization is limited because the sample consisted of volunteers.
- This is an observational study because sex was recorded, so no causal comparison is possible.
- This is a simple random sample of all adults and therefore generalizes broadly.
- Blocking was not used because everyone received either chia or placebo.

Feedback: Treatment was randomly assigned within male and female groups, so sex is a blocking variable. Random assignment supports causality, while volunteer sampling limits generalization.

### 6. Rat study: study design

What type of study is the urban rat investigation?

- **Correct:** An observational study, because researchers analyzed existing city records and did not assign temperature conditions or other treatments.
- A randomized experiment, because cities were randomly assigned to temperature trends.
- A census, because every rat in the 16 cities was counted.
- A matched-pairs experiment, because each city was assigned a control city.

Feedback: The researchers observed records and city conditions without imposing treatments, so this is an observational study.

### 7. Rat study: cases and variables

Suppose the dataset has one row for each city-year. Which statement correctly identifies the cases and variables?

- **Correct:** The cases are city-year observations; variables could include city, year, number of reports, temperature, and other city characteristics.
- The cases are only individual rats; the only variable is city.
- The cases are temperature values; the variables are the 16 cities.
- The cases are researchers; complaints are not a variable because they are a proxy.

Feedback: What counts as a case depends on the data structure. With one row per city-year, each city-year is a case and the columns are variables recorded for it.

### 8. Rat study: sampling and generalization

The researchers analyzed long-running records from 16 cities that were not described as a random sample of all cities. What is the most defensible conclusion?

- **Correct:** The results provide evidence about the studied cities, but generalizing the same pattern to all cities requires caution.
- Because 16 is larger than 10, the findings automatically represent every city.
- The findings apply to every city because the records cover multiple years.
- The study cannot describe the 16 cities because the sample was not random.

Feedback: A nonrandom set of cities can still be informative about those cities, but representativeness of a broader population is uncertain.

### 9. Rat study: proxy measurement and bias

Which change could cause reported rat complaints to increase even if the underlying number of rats did not increase?

- **Correct:** A city launches an easier-to-use complaint-reporting app and more residents begin submitting reports.
- Every neighborhood uses the same standardized direct rat-counting protocol each year.
- The actual rat population rises while reporting behavior stays unchanged.
- Researchers correct a typographical error in the study title.

Feedback: Complaint counts are a proxy. Easier reporting can increase the recorded value even when the underlying rat population is unchanged.
