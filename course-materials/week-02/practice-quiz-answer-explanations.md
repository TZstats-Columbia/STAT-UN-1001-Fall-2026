## Question 1. OpenIntro 1.6 – Stealers and study components

In a study, 129 UC Berkeley undergraduates identified themselves as lower or higher social class. Each student could take candies that had been described as intended for children, and each later reported the number taken. Which option correctly identifies the study components?

Correct answer:
The research question concerns an association between perceived social class and candies taken; the subjects are the 129 undergraduates; social class is categorical and number of candies is numerical and discrete.

Why:
The investigators recorded perceived social class and a count of candies for each of 129 student participants. Social class was not randomly assigned, so the study examines association rather than a causal effect of social class.

## Question 2. OpenIntro 1.17 – Relaxing after work

A random sample of 1,155 Americans reported how many hours they had to relax after an average workday. The sample mean was 1.65 hours. Which classification is correct?

Correct answer:
A sampled American is an observation; hours of relaxation is a variable; 1.65 is a sample statistic; the mean for all Americans is a population parameter.

Why:
Observations are the sampled people, the recorded characteristic is the variable, a value calculated from the sample is a statistic, and the corresponding population quantity is a parameter.

## Question 3. OpenIntro 1.26 – Family size

To estimate household size, a researcher randomly samples elementary-school students and asks each student's family size. What is the main problem with this design?

Correct answer:
It will tend to overestimate average household size because larger households can contribute more students and therefore have more opportunities to enter the sample.

Why:
Random selection from a list of students is not the same as random selection of households. Households with more eligible children are more likely to be represented.

## Question 4. OpenIntro 1.30 – Vitamin supplements

Researchers assigned 400 university volunteers to placebo or one of three vitamin-C treatments. Tablets looked identical. Participants did not know their treatment, researchers assessing outcomes did not know, but nurses distributing pills did know. Which description is most accurate?

Correct answer:
This is an experiment; treatment group is explanatory; cold duration or severity is the response; participants were blinded, but the study was not fully double-blind because the nurses knew assignments.

Why:
Researchers imposed treatments, making this an experiment. Identical tablets can blind participants, but staff members who know assignments prevent full double-blinding.

## Question 5. OpenIntro 1.37 – Chia seeds and weight loss

Researchers recruited 38 men and 38 women who volunteered. Within each sex, participants were randomly assigned to receive chia seeds or a placebo. Which conclusion about the design is correct?

Correct answer:
This is a randomized experiment blocked by sex; it can support a causal comparison for participants, but generalization is limited because the sample consisted of volunteers.

Why:
Treatment was randomly assigned within male and female groups, so sex is a blocking variable. Random assignment supports causality, while volunteer sampling limits generalization.

## Question 6. Rat study – Study design

What type of study is the urban rat investigation described in the quiz instructions?

Correct answer:
An observational study, because researchers analyzed existing city records and did not assign temperature conditions or other treatments.

Why:
The researchers observed records and city conditions without imposing treatments, so this is an observational study.

## Question 7. Rat study – Cases and variables

Suppose the dataset has one row for each city-year. Which statement correctly identifies the cases and variables?

Correct answer:
The cases are city-year observations; variables could include city, year, number of reports, temperature, and other city characteristics.

Why:
What counts as a case depends on the data structure. With one row per city-year, each city-year is a case and the columns are variables recorded for it.

## Question 8. Rat study – Sampling and generalization

The researchers analyzed long-running records from 16 cities that were not described as a random sample of all cities. What is the most defensible conclusion?

Correct answer:
The results provide evidence about the studied cities, but generalizing the same pattern to all cities requires caution.

Why:
A nonrandom set of cities can still be informative about those cities, but representativeness of a broader population is uncertain.

## Question 9. Rat study – Proxy measurement and bias

Which change could cause reported rat complaints to increase even if the underlying number of rats did not increase?

Correct answer:
A city launches an easier-to-use complaint-reporting app and more residents begin submitting reports.

Why:
Complaint counts are a proxy. Easier reporting can increase the recorded value even when the underlying rat population is unchanged.
