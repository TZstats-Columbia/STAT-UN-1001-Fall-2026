## Student-Facing Paragraph

In July 2026, the Associated Press reported that many ranchers in the Rocky Mountain region were hauling water to cattle because snowpack in the Upper Colorado River Basin had been exceptionally low. This is a strong statistics story because it connects a personal, easy-to-picture problem to several kinds of data: snowpack measurements, historical comparisons, percentiles, streamflow forecasts, and decisions about herd size. Students can ask how scientists decide that a year is “record low,” what counts as a fair comparison with past years, and how one climate indicator turns into practical choices on farms and ranches.

## Synopsis And Course Fit

This story works for *Introduction to Statistical Reasoning* because it shows how summary measurements translate into real-world decisions. The AP’s July 25, 2026 story focused on ranchers in and around Montrose, Colorado who were making repeated trips to haul water after natural sources dried up. The reporting tied those experiences to official snowpack and drought information rather than treating them as isolated anecdotes.

NRCS reported in April 2026 that western snowpack was at or near record lows, with 1,575 April 1 snow-water-equivalent measurements across western monitoring networks. According to NRCS, 80% of those measurements were below the 20th percentile, 70% were below the 10th percentile, and 65% set or tied record lows. That gives students a concrete example of how a broad environmental claim is built from many local observations and then summarized.

The story also invites careful thinking about representation and uncertainty. A few ranchers’ experiences are not a random sample of every rancher in the West, but those experiences become more informative when paired with systematic snowpack and drought monitoring. Students can discuss the difference between anecdote and measurement, how historical baselines matter, and why a “record low” condition still does not predict identical outcomes for every place or every producer.

## Suggestions For Class Activities

### From Site Measurements To A Headline

Give students a simplified table of snowpack readings from several sites and ask them to decide whether a headline like “record low snowpack” is justified and what evidence they would want to see.

### Anecdote Or Systematic Data?

Put side by side a rancher interview and an NRCS summary. Ask students what each contributes and what each cannot show on its own.

### Percentile Translation

Have students explain in plain language what it means for a measurement to be below the 10th percentile or for 65% of sites to set or tie record lows.

### Planning Under Uncertainty

Ask students to imagine they manage a ranch. Using only the reported snowpack and drought information, what decisions could they justify, and what information would still be missing?

## Discussion Questions

1. Why is a “record low” statement stronger when it comes from many long-running measurement sites rather than one striking photo or one interview?
2. What does it mean to compare snowpack in 2026 with historical April 1 measurements?
3. Why are the experiences of a few ranchers informative but not automatically representative of every rancher in the region?
4. How do percentiles help describe unusually low environmental conditions?
5. Why might two ranchers in the same general drought still experience different levels of hardship?
6. What is the difference between observed low snowpack and a forecast about future water shortages?

## Example Assessment Instruments

### Short Answer

Explain why “65% of sites set or tied record lows” is more informative than saying simply “it felt dry this year.”

### Concept Check

If a site’s snowpack is below the 10th percentile, that means:

- A. it is higher than 90% of past values
- B. it is lower than about 90% of past values
- C. it is exactly 10% of normal snow
- D. it guarantees a drought emergency

Correct answer: B.

### Exit Ticket

Name one reason a regional drought summary and one rancher’s experience might not match perfectly.

## Quiz Problems

### Quiz Problem 1

- Concept tested: percentile interpretation.
- Difficulty: Easy.
- Task type: interpretation.
- Prompt: What does it mean if a snowpack reading is below the 20th percentile?
- Answer: It means the reading is lower than most historical readings for that date, specifically lower than about 80% of them.

### Quiz Problem 2

- Concept tested: representation.
- Difficulty: Easy.
- Task type: critique.
- Prompt: Why can’t two rancher interviews alone establish the average impact of drought across the whole basin?
- Answer: Because the interviewed ranchers are not a random or necessarily representative sample of all ranchers in the basin.

### Quiz Problem 3

- Concept tested: summary statistics.
- Difficulty: Medium.
- Task type: interpretation.
- Prompt: NRCS said 65% of 1,575 April 1 measurements set or tied record lows. Why is that stronger evidence than one site setting a record low?
- Answer: Because it shows unusually low conditions were widespread across many measurement locations, not limited to a single outlier site.

### Quiz Problem 4

- Concept tested: forecast versus observation.
- Difficulty: Medium.
- Task type: definition.
- Prompt: What is the difference between measuring snowpack and forecasting summer water shortages?
- Answer: Snowpack is an observed current measurement, while a water-shortage forecast uses current and historical information to predict future conditions.

### Quiz Problem 5

- Concept tested: confounding.
- Difficulty: Medium.
- Task type: reasoning.
- Prompt: Two ranchers face the same regional snow drought, but one hauls much more water than the other. Give one possible reason besides snowpack.
- Answer: They may differ in access to wells, reservoir storage, herd size, pasture location, or local rainfall.

### Quiz Problem 6

- Concept tested: cautious causal reasoning.
- Difficulty: Challenging.
- Task type: short answer.
- Prompt: A student says, “Low snowpack caused every rancher in the region to haul water.” What is a better statistical response?
- Answer: Low snowpack is an important contributor, but impacts can vary by local conditions, infrastructure, herd size, management choices, and recent weather, so the same outcome will not occur for every rancher.
