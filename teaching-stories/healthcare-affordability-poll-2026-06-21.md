## Accessible Student Source

Use the AP News article. It is readable, concrete, and gives students clear percentages, subgroup comparisons, and a documented survey method.

## Student-Facing Paragraph

A new national poll says fewer than half of U.S. adults could both afford healthcare and get quality care in 2025. The article reports that 49% of adults were classified as "cost secure," down from 61% in 2022, and that affordability was lower for some groups, including younger adults and women. This is a useful statistics story because it turns a broad social concern into measurable variables, raises questions about how a survey defines a category like "cost secure," and shows how subgroup comparisons can be informative without automatically proving causes.

## Synopsis And Course Fit

This story centers on the West Health-Gallup Affordability Index, a nationally representative survey of 5,660 adults conducted from October 27 to December 22, 2025. The survey groups respondents into categories based on whether they can afford needed care and medicine and whether they report access to quality, affordable care. The reporting gives students several accessible statistics entry points: a changing headline percentage over time, differences across age, gender, race/ethnicity, and income, and a stated margin of sampling error.

The story fits *Introduction to Statistical Reasoning* especially well because it helps students separate operational definitions from everyday language. "Can afford healthcare" sounds simple, but the survey relies on several questions and a constructed index. It also supports discussion of sampling, wording, measurement, subgroup comparison, and the difference between describing disparities and explaining them.

## Suggestions For Class Activities

### Define The Variable

Ask students whether "healthcare affordability" is a single variable or a constructed measure. Then show the three categories in the Gallup source and ask what is gained and lost by collapsing many experiences into one label.

### Poll Reading Exercise

Give students the sample size and margin of error from the article. Ask what claims seem well supported for the full sample and which claims would require more caution for smaller subgroups.

### Subgroup Comparison

Have students compare the reported "cost secure" percentages for young adults, older adults, men, and women. Ask them to write one sentence that is statistically accurate and one sentence that overreaches.

### Survey Critique

Ask groups to brainstorm how self-reported affordability could differ from objective financial records, and whether that makes the measure less useful or just different.

## Example Assessment Instruments

### Short Answer

The article says 49% of U.S. adults were "cost secure" in 2025, compared with 61% in 2022. Explain what this comparison shows and name one reason to be cautious when interpreting the difference.

### Cases And Variables Prompt

For this story, identify the cases, two explanatory variables, and one response variable. Then explain whether "cost secure" is directly observed or constructed from multiple survey answers.

### Exit Ticket

Why is a margin of error relevant when interpreting a poll, and why might subgroup results still require extra caution even when the full sample is large?

## Quiz Problems

### Quiz Problem 1

- Concept tested: operational definition.
- Difficulty: Easy.
- Task type: naming concepts.
- Prompt: The label "cost secure" is built from several survey questions. What statistical idea does this illustrate?
- Answer: Operational definition or index construction.

### Quiz Problem 2

- Concept tested: representativeness.
- Difficulty: Easy.
- Task type: true/false.
- Prompt: True or false: A large sample size alone guarantees that a poll is representative of all U.S. adults.
- Answer: False. Sampling method and coverage also matter.

### Quiz Problem 3

- Concept tested: percentage-point change.
- Difficulty: Medium.
- Task type: calculation.
- Prompt: The share classified as "cost secure" fell from 61% in 2022 to 49% in 2025. What is the percentage-point change?
- Answer: 49% - 61% = -12 percentage points.

### Quiz Problem 4

- Concept tested: margin of error interpretation.
- Difficulty: Medium.
- Task type: interpreting results.
- Prompt: In one or two sentences, explain why the overall poll estimate may be fairly precise while estimates for a smaller subgroup such as young adults may be less precise.
- Answer: Smaller subgroup samples usually have more sampling variability, so their margins of error are larger even when the full sample is large.

### Quiz Problem 5

- Concept tested: causal caution.
- Difficulty: Medium.
- Task type: short answer.
- Prompt: The poll finds lower affordability among younger adults than older adults. Why is it not enough to conclude that age itself causes the difference?
- Answer: Other factors such as income, insurance coverage, employment, or health needs may differ across age groups and confound the comparison.

### Quiz Problem 6

- Concept tested: better measurement design.
- Difficulty: Challenging.
- Task type: design a study.
- Prompt: Propose one additional question or variable that would help evaluate healthcare affordability more effectively than the current index alone.
- Answer: A strong answer proposes a relevant variable such as delayed care, prescription nonadherence, out-of-pocket spending, debt, or insurance status and explains how it adds information.
