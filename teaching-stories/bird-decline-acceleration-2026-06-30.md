## Accessible Student Source

Use the AP News article. It is accessible even for students with no background in ecology and gives clear numbers and careful language about what the study did and did not show.

## Student-Facing Paragraph

A recent study reported that many North American bird populations are not just declining, but declining faster than before. The AP story says about half of the 261 bird species studied showed statistically significant losses, and more than half of the declining species were seeing those losses accelerate after 1987. This is a useful statistics story because it helps students think about long-term data, trend versus acceleration, correlation versus causation, and why scientists can find strong patterns without proving a single cause.

## Synopsis And Instructor Context

This story gives students a clean way to reason about environmental data without getting buried in technical methods. The AP article reports that researchers used North American Breeding Bird Survey data, examined 261 species from 1987 to 2021, and found that overall abundance dropped by about 15%. It also reports that the strongest acceleration of losses appeared in the Mid-Atlantic, Midwest, and California, and that places with heavier pesticide use, fertilizer use, and cropland tended to show faster declines.

For *Introduction to Statistical Reasoning*, the strongest teaching value is that the article itself models cautious language. Researchers found statistical connections between faster bird declines and agricultural intensity, but they did not claim the study proved one specific causal mechanism. That makes the story excellent for discussing association, confounding, omitted variables, ecological data, and why "more evidence" often means better description before it means stronger causal proof.

## *Introduction to Statistical Reasoning* Alignment

- Week 1: Identify the claim, the cases, and the variables.
- Week 2: Discuss observational data and the meaning of a long-running monitoring system.
- Week 3: Interpret change over time and the difference between decline and accelerating decline.
- Week 4: Practice careful language about association, confounding, and causation.
- Week 5: Use simple proportions such as "about half of species" to discuss what summaries reveal and what they hide.
- Week 6: Discuss sampling variability and measurement error in long-running wildlife surveys.
- Week 7: Compare regions or species groups with graphs, medians, and spread rather than relying on a single overall decline number.
- Week 8: Identify possible confounders when relating bird losses to cropland, pesticide use, or warming.
- Week 9: Discuss statistical significance for species-level declines and why significance is not the same as ecological importance.
- Week 10: Use the story to separate prediction questions from causal questions about where declines may worsen next.
- Week 11: Critique causal headlines and explain what experimental or stronger quasi-experimental evidence would add.
- Week 12: Revisit the story as a capstone example of longitudinal data, observational evidence, and cautious public communication.

## Suggestions For Class Activities

### Decline Versus Acceleration

Ask students to explain the difference between "bird numbers are falling" and "bird losses are accelerating." Then have them sketch two simple line graphs that show those two ideas.

### Cases And Units

Have students propose three possible units of analysis for this story: one species, one survey route-year, or one region-year. Discuss how the variables and conclusions change with each choice.

### Causal Language Revision

Give students a sentence such as "Pesticides are causing bird collapse." Ask them to rewrite it into a more defensible sentence based on the study described in the article.

### Regional Comparison

Students compare regions named in the article and discuss what extra data they would want before concluding that agriculture explains the differences.

## Discussion Questions

1. What is the main statistical claim in this story?

3. Why is this evidence observational rather than experimental?
4. What are possible confounders when comparing bird losses across regions?
5. Why does the article say agriculture is a factor without saying it is the proven cause?
6. What graph would best help a general audience understand this story?

## Example Assessment Instruments

### Short Answer

The article says about half of the 261 bird species studied showed statistically significant losses. Explain what that means and name one thing it does not mean.

### Study Design Prompt

Suppose you want to build a dataset from this story. Define the cases, identify one response variable and three explanatory variables, and name one reason a causal conclusion would still require caution.

### Exit Ticket

Why is "areas with more intense agriculture had faster bird declines" not the same as "agriculture alone caused the declines"?

## Quiz Problems

### Quiz Problem 1

- Concept tested: trend versus acceleration.
- Difficulty: Easy.
- Task type: multiple choice.
- Prompt: Which statement best describes the difference between decline and accelerating decline? A. Decline means numbers go down; accelerating decline means they go down faster over time. B. Decline means numbers go up; accelerating decline means they go down. C. They mean exactly the same thing. D. Accelerating decline means the data are experimental.
- Answer: A. Decline means numbers go down; accelerating decline means they go down faster over time.

### Quiz Problem 2

- Concept tested: observational study.
- Difficulty: Easy.
- Task type: multiple choice.
- Prompt: Why is this study observational rather than experimental? A. Researchers randomly assigned pesticides to regions. B. Researchers only studied one bird species. C. Researchers analyzed survey data without randomly assigning agricultural conditions. D. Researchers proved agriculture caused bird decline.
- Answer: C. Researchers analyzed survey data without randomly assigning agricultural conditions.

### Quiz Problem 3

- Concept tested: association versus causation.
- Difficulty: Medium.
- Task type: multiple choice.
- Prompt: The article says places with heavier pesticide use tended to show faster bird declines. Which is the most careful interpretation? A. Pesticides were proven to be the only cause. B. The data show an association, but other explanations may also matter. C. The result must be due to random guessing. D. Bird decline can be ignored because it was not measured directly.
- Answer: B. The data show an association, but other explanations may also matter.

### Quiz Problem 4

- Concept tested: confounding.
- Difficulty: Medium.
- Task type: short answer.
- Prompt: In one or two sentences, name one possible confounder that could affect the relationship between agricultural intensity and bird decline, and explain why it matters.
- Answer: A strong answer names a factor such as habitat loss, urbanization, or regional climate and explains that it could influence both farming intensity and bird decline.

### Quiz Problem 5

- Concept tested: proportion interpretation.
- Difficulty: Medium.
- Task type: calculation.
- Prompt: If about half of 261 studied species showed statistically significant losses, about how many species is that?
- Answer: About 130 or 131 species.

### Quiz Problem 6

- Concept tested: stronger ecological design.
- Difficulty: Challenging.
- Task type: design a study.
- Prompt: Propose one additional dataset or comparison that would strengthen a claim about why bird declines are accelerating.
- Answer: A strong answer adds richer habitat, land-use, or climate data or a more targeted regional comparison that could reduce confounding.

### Mini-Rubric

- Correctly identifies an observational study.
- Distinguishes decline from accelerating decline.
- Notes at least one plausible confounder or omitted variable.
- Uses causal language cautiously.
